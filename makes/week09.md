## Process Notes
✦ How did you make this? I started with one base image prompt for the AI to generate and then changed one visual detail at a time, such as lighting, makeup, color, texture, and symbols.
✦ What tools did you use? Nano Banana
✦ What decisions did you make? I decided to keep the same eye image as the base while changing specific variables, then selected the three strongest final images based on which ones felt most visually meaningful.

## Reflection
For this project, I built a generative image system using the AI image generator, Nano Banana. I started by creating a base prompt that described an extreme close up beauty photograph of a human eye with pastel pink eyeshadow, long lashes, glossy skin, and a flower shape reflected in the pupil. This base image acted as the foundation for the entire system. Instead of generating completely different images each time, I created a rule based process where I changed only one variable at a time while keeping the rest of the prompt consistent. This allowed me to explore systematic variation and to have a reason for each prompt, rather than just random generation. The main parameters I controlled were eyeshadow color, makeup style, lighting, photography style, texture, eye color, and the symbol reflected in the pupil. For example, I changed the eyeshadow from pink to gold and blue, changed the makeup style to Y2K, changed the lighting from soft to dark, dramatic, and colored gel lighting, and changed the photography style to 1970s Vogue film photography and black and white film. I also experimented with texture by creating a wet look and adding water droplets to the eyelashes. Another important variable was the symbol inside the pupil, which I changed from a flower to a star, flame, and heart outline. By controlling these parameters one at a time, I created a system that generated many variations from a single original image.
In total, I generated 15 images. After a certain point, the images began to feel repetitive and visually static, which helped me understand the idea of the exhaustion of novelty. Even though the AI could generate endless variations, not every image felt meaningful or visually successful. Because of this, my role shifted from generating images to curating and selecting the strongest results.

## Artist Statement
This project made me think about where creativity actually happens when AI can generate endless images. I made creative decisions at several specific moments in the process. First, I chose the subject and created the base prompt, which determined the foundation for every image that followed. Then, I decided which variables to change: color, lighting, style, texture, and the symbol inside the pupil. Another important creative decision was deciding when to stop generating images and begin curating. Finally, I made creative decisions when I selected which images were the “best” and most meaningful to include in the final set. If someone saw my curated images without explanation, they might assume the AI generated them at random, but my top 3 selection demonstrates my human decision making. I chose images that were visually distinct from one another and clearly showed different variables, demonstrating intentional selection instead of random output.
“Pure” AI creativity would be endless generation without any selection or stopping point, while “pure” human creativity would involve making something entirely by hand with full control over every decision. My work falls somewhere in between. The AI generated the images, but I created the system, chose the variables, and selected the final images. But, at a certain point, I did feel overwhelmed by how many images I could continue generating. The images began to feel repetitive, and that is when I knew it was time to stop and start curating. This made me realize that creativity in generative systems is not just about making images, but about making decisions. I could have definitely kept creating images, but I felt that the images Nano Banana was generating toward the end were bland, and that AI was starting to add its own stylistic changes instead of what I had prompted it to.
